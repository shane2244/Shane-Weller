                         Tarot Play Therapy: 
“My approach combines elements of Jungian psychology, Tarot symbolism, guided reflection inspired by hypnotherapy, and principles commonly used in psychotherapy. I don't use Tarot to predict the future. I use it as a symbolic language that helps people project their thoughts, emotions, and life experiences onto images, making difficult conversations feel safer and more natural.What surprised me most was how effective this became with adult men. Many men spend years, sometimes decades, building emotional defenses to survive pain, trauma, shame, rejection, or expectations about masculinity. Direct questions often lead to guarded answers. But when the conversation shifts toward symbols, archetypes, stories, and images, those defenses can soften. Instead of feeling interrogated, they begin exploring. The cards become mirrors rather than answers. People often discover emotions they didn't realize they were carrying. They connect memories, recognize behavioral patterns, grieve losses they never processed, and begin telling stories they've never shared with anyone. The transformation isn't created by the cards themselves. It's created because the symbolism provides a psychologically safe doorway into conversations that otherwise might never happen. The results I've witnessed have been both astonishing and heartbreaking. Again and again, I've watched people who arrived emotionally closed off leave feeling lighter, more hopeful, and more connected to themselves. In many cases, what they seemed to need most wasn't someone to fix them. They needed someone who could truly listen without judgment. Whether every metaphysical explanation surrounding Tarot can be scientifically validated is, to me, a separate question from whether the process helps people. Psychology has long recognized the power of symbolism, narrative, imagination, projection, and archetypes. If a symbolic framework consistently helps people access buried emotions, process trauma, and move toward healing, then it deserves careful study rather than immediate dismissal. I'm not asking people to abandon science. I'm suggesting we investigate why this works. If symbolic tools can help bypass emotional defenses and create meaningful therapeutic conversations, they may represent an overlooked bridge between traditional psychology and the symbolic traditions that human cultures have used for thousands of years. Sometimes healing doesn't begin when someone finally has the right answer. Sometimes healing begins when someone finally feels heard.”
